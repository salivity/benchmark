How VRAM Thermal Pads Affect Hardware Benchmarking

This article explores how the thickness and thermal conductivity of thermal pads on Video Random Access Memory (VRAM) chips directly impact computer hardware benchmarking. During intensive benchmarking tests, VRAM generates significant heat that must be transferred efficiently to the graphics card's heatsink. Choosing the correct thermal pad specifications prevents thermal throttling, increases overclocking headroom, and ensures accurate, peak performance scores, whereas incorrect specifications can ruin benchmark runs and cause severe hardware overheating.

Thermal Conductivity and Heat Transfer

Thermal conductivity, measured in Watts per meter-Kelvin (W/mK), dictates how efficiently a thermal pad transfers heat from the VRAM chips to the GPU cooler. Factory-installed thermal pads usually range from 3 W/mK to 6 W/mK, while high-performance aftermarket pads offer 12 W/mK to 15 W/mK or higher.

During a benchmark, high-conductivity pads draw heat away from the memory modules rapidly. This keeps VRAM junction temperatures well below their thermal throttling limits, which is typically between 105°C and 110°C for modern memory like GDDR6X. Lower temperatures allow the memory to maintain stable, high clock speeds throughout prolonged stress tests, resulting in higher and more consistent benchmark scores.

The Precision of Thermal Pad Thickness

Thermal pad thickness, measured in millimeters, is even more critical than conductivity. Graphics cards are engineered with strict tolerances, requiring exact pad thicknesses (such as 1.0mm, 1.5mm, or 2.0mm) to bridge the gap between the VRAM and the heatsink. Getting this measurement wrong will immediately ruin a benchmark in one of two ways:

  • Pads are too thin: The thermal pad will not make full physical contact with both the VRAM chip and the heatsink. Air acts as a thermal insulator, so the trapped air gap prevents heat transfer. The VRAM will rapidly overheat within seconds of starting a benchmark, triggering severe thermal throttling, screen artifacting, or an immediate system crash.
  • Pads are too thick: Excessively thick pads, or high-density pads that do not compress easily, will physically block the heatsink from sitting flush against the main GPU core. While the VRAM might make great contact and stay cool, the GPU core will lose contact with the cooler. In a benchmark scenario, the GPU core will instantly hit its thermal limit, dropping its clock speeds to the absolute minimum and destroying the benchmark score.

Impact on Overclocking and Error Correction

Benchmarking frequently involves overclocking hardware to achieve record scores. Upgrading to thermal pads with the correct thickness and high thermal conductivity lowers baseline temperatures, providing the thermal headroom necessary to push VRAM frequencies higher without crashing.

Furthermore, modern VRAM utilizes error-correction mechanisms. When VRAM gets too hot—even before hitting its hard thermal throttling limit—the memory becomes unstable and generates data errors. Instead of crashing, the GPU uses valuable clock cycles to resend data and correct these errors. This creates a "soft throttle" where benchmark scores drop even though clock speeds appear high. Proper thermal pads keep the VRAM cool enough to prevent memory errors, ensuring that every megahertz of an overclock translates directly into a higher benchmark score.
