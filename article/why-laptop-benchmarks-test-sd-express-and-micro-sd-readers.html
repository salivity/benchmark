Why Laptop Benchmarks Test SD Express and microSD Readers

Benchmarking creator laptops requires evaluating every component that impacts a professional's daily workflow, which is why testing microSD and SD Express card readers is a mandatory part of hardware reviews. This article explains how card reader speeds directly affect media transfer times, how hardware manufacturers often hide performance bottlenecks in these ports, and why testing for modern standards like SD Express is vital for photographers, videographers, and content creators who handle massive data files.

The Data Ingestion Bottleneck

For content creators, the workflow begins with transferring data—often referred to as data ingestion. Modern cameras, drones, and audio recorders generate massive files, particularly when shooting in uncompressed RAW formats or 4K and 8K video resolutions. A single shoot can easily result in hundreds of gigabytes of data.

If a laptop has a top-tier processor and graphics card, it can edit video quickly. However, if the built-in card reader is slow, the creator has to wait hours just to move the footage onto the laptop before editing can even begin. Benchmarking these readers tells buyers exactly how much time they will spend waiting on file transfers.

Exposing Hidden Hardware Cutbacks

Laptop manufacturers frequently include SD or microSD card slots on creator laptops as a selling point, but they rarely advertise the internal wiring of those slots on the specification sheet. To cut costs, a manufacturer might install a physical card slot that supports high-speed cards, yet wire it to an older, slower internal motherboard interface, such as USB 2.0.

Without benchmarking, a buyer might assume their expensive laptop has a fast card reader, only to find out it caps file transfers at 30 megabytes per second (MB/s). Hardware reviewers test these slots to expose whether the manufacturer used a budget UHS-I interface (topping out around 104 MB/s), a faster UHS-II interface (up to 312 MB/s), or the latest SD Express technology.

The Leap to SD Express

SD Express is a major reason card reader benchmarking has become more rigorous. By utilizing PCIe and NVMe interfaces—the same technology found in modern solid-state drives (SSDs)—SD Express cards can achieve transfer speeds of nearly 1,000 MB/s to almost 4,000 MB/s, depending on the generation.

As cameras evolve to record higher bitrates, SD Express is replacing older SD standards to handle the data load. However, an SD Express card will only reach these SSD-like speeds if the laptop's card reader also supports the PCIe/NVMe protocol. If a laptop's reader does not support it, the SD Express card will backward-compatibly drop down to legacy speeds of around 100 MB/s. Benchmarking confirms whether a creator laptop is truly future-proofed for this new media standard.

The Importance of microSD for Modern Creators

While traditional photographers and cinema camera operators use full-sized SD cards, a massive segment of the creator economy relies on microSD cards. Drones, action cameras, 360-degree cameras, and compact field recorders almost exclusively use the microSD format.

Many creator laptops opt for a microSD slot instead of a full-sized SD slot to save physical space inside thin chassis designs. Testing these microSD slots is essential because these smaller cards now also support UHS-II and microSD Express speeds. Creators using drones to capture high-bitrate aerial footage need the same fast ingestion speeds as those using traditional cinema cameras.

Eliminating the Need for Dongles

A primary reason professionals buy dedicated creator laptops is convenience and portability. They want a machine that works natively with their gear in the field without requiring a bag full of external hubs, dongles, and adapters.

If a laptop's built-in card reader fails benchmark tests by delivering poor transfer speeds, the user is forced to buy and carry an external USB-C card reader to get their work done efficiently. Benchmarking holds hardware manufacturers accountable, ensuring that a built-in port on a laptop marketed to professionals actually performs at a professional level.
