Best Hardware Benchmarking Tools for Apple Silicon Macs

This article provides a direct overview of the best hardware benchmarking tools natively optimized for Apple Silicon Macs running macOS. Whether you are evaluating an M1, M2, or M3 series processor, this guide covers the essential software needed to accurately measure your Mac's CPU speed, GPU graphics rendering, thermal throttling, and SSD storage performance.

Geekbench 6 (CPU and Compute)

Geekbench 6 is the industry standard for quick, cross-platform processor benchmarking. It runs natively on Apple Silicon and measures both single-core and multi-core CPU performance using workloads that simulate real-world tasks, such as loading websites, rendering PDFs, and processing photos. It also includes a Compute benchmark that tests the performance of your Mac's integrated GPU using Apple’s native Metal API. Geekbench is ideal for quickly comparing your Mac's performance against an extensive online database of other devices.

Cinebench 2024 (Sustained CPU and GPU)

Developed by Maxon, Cinebench 2024 utilizes the Redshift rendering engine to push both the CPU and GPU to their absolute limits. Unlike Geekbench, which runs short bursts of tests, Cinebench runs a continuous, heavy workload by rendering a complex 3D scene. This makes it the best tool for testing sustained performance and identifying thermal throttling, which is particularly useful when comparing fanless Macs (like the MacBook Air) against actively cooled Macs (like the MacBook Pro or Mac Studio).

3DMark (GPU and Ray Tracing)

Long considered a staple on Windows, 3DMark is available natively on macOS for Apple Silicon. It is the premier tool for benchmarking gaming performance and high-end graphics. It includes "Wild Life Extreme" for testing standard GPU compute power and "Solar Bay," a benchmark specifically designed to test real-time ray tracing capabilities on newer Apple Silicon chips (such as the M3 series) using the Metal API.

GFXBench Metal (Graphics Stability)

GFXBench is a dedicated GPU benchmark tool that natively supports Apple’s Metal framework. It offers a variety of high-level and low-level graphics tests, including Aztec Ruins. Beyond measuring raw frame rates, GFXBench is exceptionally good at testing battery drain and long-term graphics stability, showing you how your Mac's GPU performs as the system heats up over repeated runs.

Blender Benchmark (3D Rendering)

For professionals working in 3D modeling and animation, the official Blender Benchmark is the most accurate indicator of real-world hardware performance. It natively supports Apple Silicon CPUs and leverages the Metal backend for GPU rendering. The tool downloads three distinct Blender scenes (monster, junkshop, and classroom) and measures how many samples per minute your hardware can process.

Blackmagic Disk Speed Test (Storage)

Blackmagic Disk Speed Test is a fast, user-friendly utility for benchmarking internal and external SSDs. With a single click, it writes and reads large blocks of data to your drive, displaying the continuous read and write speeds in megabytes per second (MB/s). It also features a grid that tells you what video resolutions and framerates your storage is fast enough to handle, making it a favorite for video editors.

AmorphousDiskMark (Detailed Storage)

While Blackmagic is great for sequential speed tests, AmorphousDiskMark provides a much deeper analysis of your SSD's hardware capabilities. Functioning as the macOS equivalent to Windows' CrystalDiskMark, this tool tests both sequential read/write speeds (large files) and random 4K read/write speeds (small, fragmented files). Random 4K performance is the metric that best represents how fast your Mac will feel during everyday operating system tasks and app launches.
